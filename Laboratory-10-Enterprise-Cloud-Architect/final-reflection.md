### Final Reflection

Before this course, I thought cloud computing was just "computers on the
internet" that companies rent. Over ten missions, I learned that the cloud is
made of many parts that work together: virtual machines, networks, containers,
firewalls, and scripts. Each mission taught me one of these parts, and every
mission helped me with the next one.

## Missions 1 to 9: Building the Skills

In the early missions, I learned the basics of cloud computing, virtualization,
and working with Linux in the terminal. In the middle missions, I learned about
networking, containers, Docker, and storage. [Add one or two sentences about
the mission that was hardest for you, and what you learned from it.]

Some of these missions did not go smoothly, and that was where I learned the
most. Fixing problems taught me to read error messages, test one thing at a
time, and look for another way when the first way does not work.

## Mission 10: The Enterprise Cloud Architect

Mission 10 brought all of these together. We built an Ubuntu Server VM in VirtualBox, deployed WordPress and MariaDB with Docker Compose, locked the server down with a Default Deny firewall, and scheduled a nightly database backup. The hardest part was connecting the pieces, because the network, the containers, the firewall, and the backup all had to work together. Troubleshooting skills from earlier missions helped, for example when a container image could not be pulled in Mission 5 and we had to find a working alternative.

This course changed how I see technology. Before, I only used websites and
apps. Now I understand the servers, networks, and security behind them. Mission
10 was the hardest mission, but it was also the most useful, because it showed
me that I can join many small skills into one working system. I want to keep
learning and use these skills in my future IT career.
